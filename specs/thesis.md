# Thesis

## The broken status quo

Identifying a startup idea with genuine market traction is not a creative problem — it's a research problem that most founders do badly or skip entirely. The typical process is: founder has a hunch, googles a bit, finds one or two competitors, declares "the market exists," and starts building. They discover the gap was fake, the competitor already solved it, or the job they're trying to replace isn't actually done digitally six months in.

The failure is structural: good idea validation requires cross-referencing job market data, competitor landscapes, regulatory constraints, technology readiness, and willingness-to-pay signals simultaneously. No human does all of this systematically. Most don't do even two of them.

For the specific niche of "AI agent for rent" — replacing a specific knowledge-worker task with a deployed AI agent as a productized service — the research problem is even harder. You need to find jobs that are: (a) real and recurring, (b) complex enough to have a moat, (c) purely or mostly digital in I/O, (d) not already targeted by a funded competitor, and (e) not in a regulated space that blocks AI substitution.

## Why existing solutions fail

- **ChatGPT / Claude ad-hoc prompting**: generates ideas fluently but hallucinates market data, doesn't check real competitors, and has no structured output. You get confident-sounding noise.
- **YC Startup School / idea frameworks**: teach how to think about ideas but do zero research for you. You still have to do the legwork.
- **Existing market research tools (Crunchbase, CB Insights, Similarweb)**: cover funded companies well but miss the absence of competition — the thing that actually matters for gap-finding. They tell you what exists, not what doesn't.
- **LinkedIn / job board scraping**: gives raw job data but no synthesis — no creativity layer, no competitor check, no "could AI do this?" scoring.
- **Generic idea generators**: produce output like "build an app for dog walkers." No market validation, no gap analysis, no critique.

None of them combine: (1) constraint-driven search, (2) real-time web research, (3) competitor gap analysis, and (4) adversarial self-critique in one automated loop.

## The signal

- The "vibe coding" and "AI agent" wave has created massive founder appetite for AI-native products but most people don't know *which* specific task to target.
- The number of productized AI services (AI agent for X) is growing fast, but the hit rate remains low because founders pick ideas based on what sounds cool, not what has an empty market.
- Switzerland specifically has high labor costs, strong digitization in professional services, and a culture of precision work — a good testbed for high-value digital task automation.
- Google Gemini, Vertex AI, and Agent Builder are maturing fast — the tooling to build targeted AI agents is now accessible to small teams.

## The claim

This agent does the research a founder is too lazy or busy to do: given constraints, it searches for real jobs, identifies which ones AI could replace, checks whether anyone is already doing it, critiques each idea adversarially, and surfaces only the ones that pass the filter. It produces ranked, justified startup leads — not vibes.
